By the Skitovich–Darmois theorem, forward-noise Gaussianity is sample-wise equivalent to reverse residual independence—identifying causal direction or abstaining on Gaussian data without fragile normality pre-tests.

01 · Skitovich–Darmois

Normal–Independence Duality

Theorem 4.2 proves reverse predictor $Y$ and residual $\epsilon_X$ are independent iff $X \perp\!\!\!\perp \epsilon_Y$ and both follow normal laws.

$$Y \perp\!\!\!\perp \epsilon_X \iff \big(X \perp\!\!\!\perp \epsilon_Y \;\wedge\; X,\epsilon_Y \sim \mathcal{N}\big)$$
02 · Theorem 4.3

Consistent Test Equivalence

Forward Gaussianity tests $\varphi_{\mathrm{GT}i}$ and reverse independence tests $\varphi_{\mathrm{IT}i}$ match across samples, replacing fragile Shapiro–Wilk or JB checks.

$$\varphi_{\mathrm{IT}i}(S) = \varphi_{\mathrm{GT}i}(S), \quad \forall S,\; i \in \{1, 2\}$$
03 · Algorithm 1

Four-Way Decision Rule

Testing $\varphi_1 = \mathbb{I}(Y \perp\!\!\!\perp \epsilon_X)$ and $\varphi_2 = \mathbb{I}(X \perp\!\!\!\perp \epsilon_Y)$ orients non-Gaussian pairs and abstains cleanly when noise is Gaussian.

$$(\varphi_1,\varphi_2) \mapsto \{(0,1)\!:\! X\!\to\! Y,\;(1,0)\!:\! Y\!\to\! X,\;(1,1)\!:\! \mathcal{N},\;(0,0)\!:\! \mathrm{Inc.}\}$$

Scope

Assumptions & diagnostic boundaries

Standing structural premises: Linearity, acyclicity, and exogeneity (no hidden confounders) remain required; nonlinear or confounded pairs typically trigger $(\varphi_1, \varphi_2) = (0, 0)$ (inconclusive).

Data-driven Gaussian abstention: Non-Gaussian noise is still necessary for identifiability, but no longer assumed ex ante—when disturbances are Gaussian, $(\varphi_1, \varphi_2) = (1, 1)$ abstains cleanly.
