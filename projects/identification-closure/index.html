Iterative Identification Closure: Amplifying Causal Identifiability in Linear SEMs

Ziyi Ding1, Xiao-Ping Zhang1,*

* Corresponding author.

Standard Half-Trek Criterion (HTC) resolves all parents of a node at once. Iterative Identification Closure (IIC) substitutes seed coefficients and propagates Reduced HTC to amplify partial knowledge into near-global identifiability.

Two-Phase Architecture

Turn sparse causal seeds into a monotone fixed-point closure.

01 · Composable Seeds

Modular Seed Interface

Any sound source—instrumental variables, $\mathrm{do}(v)$ interventions, non-Gaussianity, or domain knowledge—initializes $\mathcal{S}_0$ and composes monotonically.

$$\mathcal{I}_0 = \mathcal{S}_0 \cup \{e \in D : e \text{ is HTC-identifiable}\}$$
02 · Theorem 4.3

Reduced HTC Substitution

Subtracting known parent effects $K \subseteq \mathrm{pa}(i)$ shrinks unknown parents to $R = \mathrm{pa}(i)\setminus K$ while preserving generic full rank of $J = [\Sigma_{w_l, r_m}]$.

$$X_i' = X_i - \sum_{k \in K} B_{ki} X_k = \sum_{r \in R} B_{ri} X_r + \varepsilon_i$$
03 · Fixed-Point Closure

Node-Wise Optimal Limit

Iterating Reduced HTC reaches a unique least fixed point in $\le |D|$ rounds ($\le 2$ empirically), strictly subsuming HTC and ancestor decomposition.

$$\mathcal{I}_{k+1} = \mathcal{I}_k \cup \{j \to i : R = \mathrm{pa}(i)\setminus K \text{ satisfies Reduced HTC}\}$$

Scope

Optimality class & misspecification profile

Node-wise polynomial boundary: IIC is optimal within node-wise HTC rules (Theorem B.2); residual gap edges satisfy $|R| \ge 2$ and $|R \cap \mathrm{sib}(i)| \ge 1$ ($78.7\%$ have $|R|=2, |R \cap \mathrm{sib}(i)|=1$), requiring extra seeds or NP-hard cross-node Gröbner solvers.

Graph error resilience: Under $30\%$ structural perturbation (Table 12), precision stays $\ge 96.4\%$ and recall $\ge 96.5\%$; omitted latent confounders represent the main precision risk, while extra bidirected edges safely lower recall only.
